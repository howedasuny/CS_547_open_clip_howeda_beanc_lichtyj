  - Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the approach now that you have used it directly

# Presentation Content (35%)

Your slides should be formatted as follows and contain the content listed below. Slide counts are recommendations, not restrictions. Also, percentages here are of the CONTENT grade:

- (5%) Introduction (~1 slide)
  - Explain the overall problem
  - Explain why it is challenging
  - Explain why it is important
- (15%) Original Method (~4-5 slides)
  - Explain what the approach is in more detail than the SOA presentation
  - Talk about architectures and pipelines, where appropriate/relevant
  - Show appropriate diagrams sufficiently large enough to read important details
- (20%) New Method (~1-2 slides)
  - Talk about your modifications to the approach, whether they are code modifications and/or using a new dataset
- (20%) Original Experiments and Results (~1-2 slides)
  - Present the results you got from running the approach and contrast them with the original paper results (numbers AND images if relevant)
  - If there are significant differences, discuss why you think that might be the case
- (20%) New Experiments and Results (~1-2 slides)
  - Present your NEW experimental results and contrast them with the original results (again, numbers AND images if relevant)
  - Discuss/interpret your results
- (10%) Conclusion (~1 slide)
  - Talk about the advantages and disadvantages of the approach overall now that you have had a chance to work with it directly.
- (5%) References (~1 slide)
  - Academic papers and journals (as well as conference talks) should be cited in the format used in IEEE papers, to wit: [1] Author, author, and author. “Title”, Journal or conference name, year.
  - If you have additional references (including image references), these must be cited as well:
    - Websites can be cited as:
      - [1] Author (if available), "Title", year. [Online]. Available: http://Website URL. [Accessed: 05-Apr-2022].
- (5%) AI Usage (~1 slide)
  - List any AI agents and briefly how they were used
  - Include links to chat logs

Some additional guidance:

- Your complete slide deck will probably be approximately 11 - 15 slides.
- Your audience consists of computer science professionals who are familiar with basic computer graphics and/or computer vision BUT do not specialize in it.
- When describing the methods in each paper, the level of detail should be enough that the audience could POTENTIALLY implement it, but they may get stuck on a few details.
- Do NOT copy whole sections of the paper(s) into your slides.
- Do not have so little text per slide as to be totally incomprehensible.
- Avoid full sentences where possible.
- Please check your slides for spelling and grammar mistakes.
- Please make sure you use a font size that is readable in a classroom presentation.

Upload your presentation slides to Brightspace for your group before the due date. You should ONLY have to upload it from ONE member of the group.


# Presentation Skills (25%)

Your presentation is expected to take about 12 to 15 minutes (plus a minute for questions). You have a hard stop at 18 minutes. Presenters that exceed this time limit will be told to stop, and this will be reflected in your grade.

Similarly, if you are significantly UNDER time (e.g., under 8 minutes), this too will be reflected in your grade.

Presenters should also:

- Introduce themselves
- Be clear and understandable
- Be professional
- Be engaging
- Be reasonably knowledgeable and confident in the material they are presenting (so practice ahead of time)

Presenters should also take turns presenting and have approximately equal time presenting.
